# Manuscript figures

Generated by `python scripts/make_manuscript_figures.py` from the repository root. Each figure is saved as a vector PDF (for the manuscript) and a 300 dpi PNG (for slides). Widths: Figures 1–2 are single-column (3.5 in) and Figures 3–4 are double-column (7 in). The numbers behind every panel are in `figure_data/`. All results are exploratory and descriptive, not causal.

Inputs (read-only): `Data/football_modeling_data.csv` (the partner's 2025–26 football NIL sample, n = 100) and Sam's ESPN/SportsDataverse layers under `Data/private/` (FBS rosters for 2015–2026, D-I basketball player-season minutes for 2015–2026, and the 2025–26 D-I team list).

## Figure 1. Transfers into a roster rose every year after NIL

`fig1_transfer_share.pdf`. Share of each season's roster made up of players who were on a different team the previous season, for FBS football (players rostered for 2+ games) and D-I men's basketball (players with any minutes). Transfers are identified by ESPN athlete ID. The football share counts FBS-to-FBS moves only. The dashed line marks the NCAA interim NIL policy (July 1, 2021), which arrived together with the one-time transfer exception, so the two can't be separated in this figure. Basketball seasons are labeled by the year they end. Fall 2026 football is mid-season. Data: `figure_data/roster_trends.csv`.

## Figure 2. Longer college careers came mostly from the COVID extra year

`fig2_fifth_year_share.pdf`. Share of each roster in its 5th or later recorded college season, counting recorded seasons back to 2015 (so the series starts in 2019). The shaded band marks seasons when cohorts given the 2020 COVID eligibility waiver were still on rosters. The share rose with the waiver and is now falling back, sharply in basketball (11.3% in 2024–25 to 3.9% in 2025–26). So far this doesn't show a lasting NIL effect on time in college. Recorded seasons are a lower bound, since redshirt years without minutes aren't counted in basketball. Data: `figure_data/roster_trends.csv`.

## Figure 3. Followers predict NIL valuation, but only through the top tier

`fig3_followers_vs_valuation.pdf`. (a) Instagram followers against NIL valuation for the football sample, both on log scales, with least-squares fits for all players and for players outside the top valuation tier. (b) Follower elasticity, the coefficient on ln(followers) with ln(valuation) as the outcome, with 95% CIs. The partner model (HC1) reproduces the reported 0.207. The other rows use HC3 errors. Each 10× increase in followers goes with about 1.7× the valuation. The estimate disappears once the top tier is excluded (0.03, p = 0.25). The sample was stratified on valuation, which is also the outcome, so this needs discussion in the limitations. * p < 0.05. Data: `figure_data/follower_elasticity.csv`.

## Figure 4. Recruiting stars barely predict NIL value

`fig4_recruiting_stars.pdf`. (a) NIL valuation by high-school star rating. Boxes show the median and IQR, and points are individual players. 4-star players have the lowest median. (b) Spearman correlation of each candidate predictor with valuation, with 95% bootstrap CIs (2,000 resamples, seed 510). PPA treats exact zeros as missing (n = 59), because many zeros are unmatched players rather than true zeros. The partner's model includes `Freshman_X_Stars` without a Star Rating main effect. Adding the main effect (`figure_data/stars_main_effect_models.csv`) moves the follower coefficient from 0.207 to 0.230, and stars themselves aren't significant (p = 0.28). Data: `figure_data/spearman_with_valuation.csv`.

## Data caveats that affect these figures

See the data audit for details. The NIL valuations are a 2026 post-portal snapshot with no recorded date. Follower counts have no recorded date or handle. Star ratings have no named source. FBS membership is fixed to 2025.
