Generated by `python scripts/make_manuscript_figures.py` from the repository root. Each figure is saved as a vector PDF and a 300 dpi PNG. Since November 2 2026 every figure is a plain-language bar chart for a general audience (sans-serif, labelled values, p-values in words). The numbers behind each figure are in `figure_data/`. All results are exploratory and descriptive, not causal.

Inputs (read-only): `Data/football_modeling_data.csv` (the partner's 2025–26 football NIL sample, n = 100) and Sam's ESPN/SportsDataverse layers under `Data/private/` (FBS rosters for 2015–2026, D-I basketball player-season minutes for 2015–2026, and the 2025–26 D-I team list).

## Figure 1. Transfers kept rising after NIL

`fig1_transfer_share`. Share of each season's roster made up of players who were on a different team the previous season, FBS football (rostered 2+ games) and D-I men's basketball (any minutes), bars by season. Transfers are identified by ESPN athlete ID; football counts FBS-to-FBS moves only. The dashed line marks the NCAA interim NIL policy (July 1 2021), which arrived together with the one-time transfer exception, so the two can't be separated. Basketball seasons are labelled by the year they end; fall 2026 football is mid-season. Data: `figure_data/roster_trends.csv`.

## Figure 2. The 5th-year bump came mostly from the COVID extra year

`fig2_fifth_year_share`. Share of each roster in its 5th or later recorded college season (seasons counted back to 2015, so the series starts in 2019). The shaded band marks seasons with COVID-waiver cohorts on rosters. The share rose with the waiver and is now falling back, sharply in basketball (11.3% in 2024–25 to 3.9% in 2025–26). Recorded seasons are a lower bound. Data: `figure_data/roster_trends.csv`.

## Figure 3. The follower link comes from the top-valued players

`fig3_followers_vs_valuation`. Extra NIL value for twice the Instagram followers (2^β − 1, from the coefficient on ln followers with ln valuation as the outcome) in five versions: partner model (HC1, β = 0.207, +15%), + stars main effect, followers only, without the 1M-follower account, and without the 25 top-tier players. Lines are 95% CIs (HC3 except the partner model); grey bars are not significant. Every full-sample version is +14% to +19%; without the top tier it is +2% (p = 0.25). The sample was stratified on valuation, which is also the outcome. Data: `figure_data/follower_elasticity.csv`.

## Figure 4. Recruiting stars don't line up with NIL value

`fig4_recruiting_stars`. Median NIL valuation by high-school star rating (3-star $500K, n = 17; 4-star $438K, n = 70; 5-star $700K, n = 13). The groups differ (Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.001) but not in star order: 5-star vs 4-star Mann–Whitney p < 0.001, 3-star vs 5-star p = 0.27, Spearman correlation with stars 0.08 (p = 0.42). Adding a stars main effect to the partner model gives −0.16 (p = 0.28). Data: `figure_data/spearman_with_valuation.csv`, `figure_data/stars_main_effect_models.csv`.

## Figure 5. Players with more followers have higher NIL value

`fig5_follower_quartiles`. Median NIL valuation in four equal groups of 25 players by Instagram followers (1,265–5,484; 5,626–10,700; 11,200–19,900; 20,000–1,000,000): $379K, $427K, $488K, $700K. Kruskal–Wallis p < 0.001. Unadjusted. Data: `figure_data/valuation_by_follower_quartile.csv`.

## Figure 6. What moves NIL value in the model

`fig6_feature_effects`. Each term of the position-groups model (ln valuation ~ ln(1+followers) + centred stars × freshman + position group, HC3, n = 100) as a percent change in valuation with 95% CI: followers per doubling +21% (p = 0.003), +1 star (non-freshmen) −15%, freshman at average stars −6%, extra per star for freshmen +105% (p = 0.053; CI to +326%, clipped; this is the interaction term alone, so a freshman's total change per star is 0.85 × 2.05 − 1 ≈ +75%, p = 0.12), quarterback +22%, offensive line +31%, skill offense −3% (vs defense). R² = 0.35. Drawn full-width (13.33 in) on a transparent background so each bar sits directly above its box on the model-explainer slide (`deliverables/model_explainer_slide.pptx`, slide 6 of the deck). Data: `figure_data/feature_effects_position_groups_hc3.csv`.

## Figures 7–11. Basketball and football with one model

Generated by `python scripts/make_unified_figures.py` (add `--cached` to redraw without rerunning the 1,000-draw bootstrap). Inputs: `Data/curated/basketball_instagram_100.csv`, `Data/football_modeling_data.csv`, `Data/private/basketball/player_team_season_2026.csv` (2025–26 points per game, local only) and `figure_data/roster_trends.csv`. Same model in both sports: ln(NIL value) ~ ln(1 + followers) + last-season performance percentile (within sport) + no-stats flag + years in college, HC3. Results: `figure_data/unified_model_results.json`; assumption checks, partial f² and post hoc tests: `figure_data/unified_model_diagnostics.json`.

- `fig7_transfer_rate_by_sport`: share of each roster that transferred in, by season, basketball (orange) and football (blue) side by side: 2% → 27% and 1% → 16%.
- `fig8_nil_by_follower_group_by_sport`: typical NIL value by four follower groups in each sport, grouped bars: basketball $49K → $261K, football $379K → $700K. Football was sampled from the top 1,000 valuations only, so heights are not comparable across sports. Data: `figure_data/nil_by_follower_group_by_sport.csv`.
- `fig9_follower_effect_by_sport`: twice the followers → +35% (basketball, 95% 18–54%) and +17% (football, 7–27%).
- `fig10_feature_importance`: share of explained differences (general dominance, 1,000 bootstrap draws): followers 64% / 69%, performance 12% / 20%, years in college 24% / 11%. R² 0.23 / 0.30.
- `fig11_follower_effect_top_market`: without the 25 top-valued players the follower effect is +1% (basketball, p = 0.86) and +4% (football, p = 0.05).

## Data caveats that affect these figures

The NIL valuations are a 2026 post-portal snapshot with no recorded date. Follower counts have no recorded date or handle. Star ratings have no named source. FBS membership is fixed to 2025. PPA is unverified and left out of the main model.
